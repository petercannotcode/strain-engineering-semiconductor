Model equations, assumptions & sources

Strain and valleys

x, y and z align with [100], [010] and [001]. Linear isotropic elasticity uses ν = 0.28. Biaxial: εzz = −2ν/(1−ν) ε∥; uniaxial: εyy = εzz = −ν εxx. Relative valley energy: Eᵢ = Ξu(εii − tr(ε)/3), with Ξu = 9.16 eV. A common hydrostatic shift is excluded; the mean valley edge is the energy zero. ± valleys stay degenerate.

Reciprocal vectors transform as F⁻ᵀ, where F = I + ε. Valley centers are held at 0.85 of the corresponding reduced reciprocal axis; internal strain-induced shifts are omitted. Only the real-space drawing exaggerates deformation ×12.

Populations and transport

Equal pair degeneracy and fixed density-of-states masses give Pᵢ ∝ exp[−(Eᵢ−Emin)/(kBT)], normalized over three pairs. mcond,j⁻¹ = Σᵢ Pᵢ/mᵢⱼ, using mₗ along a valley axis and mₜ across it. μ/μ₀ = mcond,0/mcond,j assumes the same constant τ. Temperature changes populations only.

Local bands use E(q) = Eᵢ + ℏ²q²/(2mᵢⱼ). These are near-minimum cuts, not a full Brillouin-zone band calculation. Fixed curvature omits strain-dependent mass changes. The displayed conductivity mass changes through valley repopulation, not a fitted change to individual valley masses. Effective mass is related to curvature by 1/m* = (1/ℏ²) d²E/dk².

Device interpretation

For v = VDS/Vov, I/I₀,sat = (μ/μ₀)(2v−v²) for 0 ≤ v ≤ 1 and μ/μ₀ above saturation. This is the ideal long-channel square-law illustration, not a calibrated MOSFET prediction. Quantum confinement, threshold shifts, scattering changes, high-field effects and strain relaxation are omitted. Linear models are illustrative at large strain.
